From the GIB League Rulebook (v2.0.1)

  • Numbers. Every pitch and swing is a whole number from 1 to 1000 (3.3). The rules put no limit on which number a pitcher may throw or how often they repeat one, so every number is always possible.
  • Difference. The result comes from the difference between pitch and swing, measured the short way round the circle, so it runs from 0 to 500. 1 and 1000 are one apart.
  • Result order. From the smallest difference up: HR, 3B, 2B, 1B, BB, DFO, WGO, SFO, K, PGO (4). A difference of 496 or more is a lineout, or a triple play with nobody out and runners on first and second (3.4).
  • Range sizes. A batter type is worth 250 points and a pitcher type 251, spread over the ten results. Added together they give the 501 differences (4). The type is the archetype plus the stars that apply: batter stars against the pitcher's hand, pitcher stars against the batter's hand, and clutch stars for both with two outs and a runner on second or third (4.1). Zero stars means the Pitcher batting type or the Position Player Pitching type.
  • Park factors. The home park multiplies the HR, 3B, 2B, 1B and BB sizes, rounded, and PGO takes up the slack so the total stays 501 (4.6).
  • Steals. A steal uses the pitcher's pitch or separate steal number against the runner's number (3.3). Steal rows in the log count here as pitches thrown.
  • Changing a pitch. A pitcher can change their number any time before the batter swings (3.2), so a read is only ever a tendency.

What this page leaves out

  • Fielder adjustments to the ranges (4.2) and the infield-in adjustment (3.3). Each moves a range by a few points.
  • Season 1 used a different set of player types, so the swing map uses Season 2 types only. Pitch habits use whichever seasons you select.
  • Auto strikeouts and auto results have no pitch number and are skipped.

How the next pitch read works

The read blends six things: the pitcher's favourite areas, their usual move from their last pitch, where they go relative to the swing they just saw, how the whole league moves from pitch to pitch, the exact numbers the league throws unusually often, and plain chance. The blend is set by replaying the pitcher's own history in order and seeing which of those would have pointed at the pitch that was actually thrown. A pitcher with little history or no real habit ends up close to an even spread. The accuracy line under the chart reports how the read would have done on their past pitches; because the blend was set on that same history, it flatters the read somewhat.

Rule 1.7 of the rulebook strongly discourages using AI or digital random number generators to choose pitches and swings. This page does not choose a number; it summarises the public log.
